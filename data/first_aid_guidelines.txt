PROTOCOL 1: SEVERE BLEEDING
- Apply firm, direct pressure to the wound using a clean cloth, gauze, or bandage.
- Do not remove the cloth if blood soaks through; place another clean cloth directly on top and keep pressing.
- Keep the injured limb elevated above heart level if there is no suspected fracture.
- Do not apply a tourniquet unless bleeding is life-threatening and cannot be controlled by direct pressure.

PROTOCOL 2: UNCONSCIOUSNESS & BREATHING CHECK
- Check if the person responds to loud voice or gentle shoulder tap.
- Tilt the head back slightly and lift the chin to open the airway.
- Look, listen, and feel for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds.
- If the person is breathing normally, place them on their side in the recovery position.
- If the person is not breathing, begin chest compressions immediately in the center of the chest at 100 to 120 beats per minute.

PROTOCOL 3: SUSPECTED FRACTURE OR SPINAL INJURY
- Do not move the injured person unless they are in immediate danger such as fire or oncoming traffic.
- Keep the head, neck, and spine completely still.
- Support the injured limb in the position found using rolled clothes or padding.
- Never attempt to straighten a broken bone.

PROTOCOL 4: BURNS
- Cool the burn immediately with cool or lukewarm running water for at least 10 to 20 minutes.
- Never apply ice, butter, oil, toothpaste, or ointments to a burn.
- Remove tight jewelry or clothing near the burn unless stuck to the skin.
- Cover loosely with a clean, dry, non-fluffy cloth.

PROTOCOL 5: CHOKING
- Encourage the person to cough forcefully if they can still speak or breathe.
- If they cannot breathe or speak, give 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
- Follow with 5 abdominal thrusts just above the navel if back blows do not clear the airway.
